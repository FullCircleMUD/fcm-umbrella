When Tim suggests an approach, evaluate whether the **concept** works before objecting.
Repeatedly I have answered the narrow literal reading ("moving the *import* saves nothing")
when the obvious intent was broader ("do this once at the top of the file"), which reads as
"that won't work" for an idea that was in fact correct.

**Why:** Tim's suggestions are usually workable in concept even when the exact mechanics need
adjusting. Answering the literal line instead of the intent costs several rounds and reads as
reflexive contradiction. His words: *"If I had a dollar for every time I suggest something and
then you tell me it won't work, and then it turns out the concept is workable..."*

**How to apply:** Lead with whether the idea works — "yes, that works, with this one change" —
then name the specific adjustment. Reserve a flat "no" for concepts that genuinely fail (see
[[feedback_lead_with_the_no]]). If the concept is sound but the code as written is not, say so
in that order: concept first, mechanics second. Never let a technical distinction stand in as a
rejection. Related: [[feedback_no_manufactured_objections]].
