Avoid phrasing that makes a small refinement or wording correction sound like a bigger problem, reversal, or admission of a mistake than it actually is. Phrases like "that changes the design, actually", "good catch", or self-flagellating corrections ("that was a poor way to say it") cause the user unnecessary alarm before they've even read the substance.

**Why:** explicitly called out — "it gives me a heart attack every bloody time." Related to [[feedback_terse_confirmations]] (verbosity/hedging) but distinct: this is about tone/framing, not length.

**How to apply:** when refining or correcting something already said, just state the refined version plainly (e.g. "simpler than that: X" or "one piece of that turned out unnecessary — Y covers it"). Don't lead with language that signals alarm or a reversal unless the change is actually substantial and the user needs to brace for it.
